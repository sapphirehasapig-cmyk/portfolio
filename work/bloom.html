Project overview / Context

‍

Many employees in Denmark bring their own lunch box every day. At the same time, companies are under pressure to reduce their carbon footprint and engage employees in sustainability.

Can one simple lunch box become a daily touchpoint between employees and the company’s green goals?
  • Company goal
    • Reduce carbon footprint and engage staff in green initiatives.
  • User problem
    • People struggle to see their personal impact
    • Don’t feel connected to the company's climate targets.

Solution Overview

‍

A Lunchbox and an App

‍

‍

‍

  • A plantable lunchbox with seeds embedded in the lid, activated when users heat their meal.
  • A companion mobile app that gives local planting tips, tracks growth, and visualises CO₂ reduction.
  • Community features and challenges that turn individual actions into shared impact at company level.

Usability Test

‍

Method: Think-aloud test with 7 participants.

Metrics: task success, time on task, error count.

‍

‍

Findings:

  1. Some users didn’t notice the tutorial.
  2. The post button in the Community wasn’t visible enough.
  3. Carbon-reduction rankings appeared in both Community and Profile, which confused people.

‍

‍

Updates：

  1. Added a clearer entry point and “Skip tutorial” option.
  2. Increased contrast and hover feedback to make the primary action stand out.
  3. Moved rankings to Profile only and simplified the layout.

What I learned

,

  • How to connect business goals (sustainability KPIs) with UX decisions instead of just making a “nice” interface.
  • How to design for a physical–digital product: the lunchbox and the app have to tell one consistent story.
  • How to use lightweight research (survey, competitor matrix, user stories) to quickly shape scope and features in a student project.
  • If I had more time, I would validate Bloom with real companies, refine the business model and prototype the employee & HR dashboards.
